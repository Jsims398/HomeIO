Topic 8 Murphy, PML — §8.7 Bound optimization · EM

Climb a hill you can't solve by climbing ones you can

EM maximizes a likelihood with hidden variables by repeating two easy steps: guess the hidden parts, then refit as if the guess were true. Each round is guaranteed not to make the fit worse.

8.7

Key points

The problem
ℓ(θ) = Σn log Σz p(xn, z | θ)

The hidden z puts a sum inside the log, so setting the gradient to zero has no closed form. If z were observed, the log would reach the Gaussian directly and the MLE would be easy.

Bound optimization (MM)
Q(θ, θt) ≤ ℓ(θ), equal at θt

Build a simpler surrogate that sits below ℓ and touches it at the current point. Maximize the surrogate. Repeat. ℓ can never go down.

EM = MM for hidden variables
E: fill in z softly · M: refit

The E step builds the surrogate by computing p(z | x, θt). The M step maximizes it, which is just a weighted version of the easy, fully observed MLE.

8.7.1

A two-bump mixture where everything is fixed except one mean, θ = μ2. That makes ℓ(θ) a curve you can see. Each E step draws the real EM lower bound under it; each M step jumps to the bound's peak.

8.7.2

Where the bound comes from

The green curve above isn't hand-drawn. It's built from any distribution qn(z) over each point's hidden label, then tightened by the E step.

8.7.2.1 · Lower bound (Jensen)
ℓ(θ) ≥ Σn Σz qn(z) log [ p(xn, z | θ) ⁄ qn(z) ] = Ł(θ, q)

Multiply and divide by q inside the log, then log is concave so log E[·] ≥ E[log ·]. The gap is exactly Σn KL(qn ‖ p(z | xn, θ)), which is never negative.

8.7.2.2 · E step
qn(z) = p(z | xn, θt)

Choose q to be the posterior over the hidden label. The KL gap drops to 0, so Ł(θt, qt) = ℓ(θt): the bound touches the curve at the current point.

8.7.2.3 · M step
θt+1 = argmaxθ Σn Eqn[ log p(xn, zn | θ) ]

The entropy part of Ł doesn't depend on θ, so we only maximize the expected complete-data log-likelihood Q(θ, θt). Then ℓ(θt+1) ≥ Ł(θt+1, qt) ≥ Ł(θt, qt) = ℓ(θt).

8.7.3

EM for a Gaussian mixture, one step at a time

Now every parameter is free: weights πk, means μk, spreads σk. The hidden variable zn is which component made point xn.

8.7.3.1 · E step: responsibilities
rnk = πk N(xn | μk, σk²) ⁄ Σj πj N(xn | μj, σj²)

Bayes' rule for each point: how much of xn does component k explain? Each row of r sums to 1.

8.7.3.2 · M step: weighted MLE
Nk = Σn rnk  ·  πk = Nk ⁄ N

μk = Σn rnk xn ⁄ Nk  and  σk² = Σn rnk (xn − μk)² ⁄ Nk. The usual mean and variance, but each point counts rnk times.
