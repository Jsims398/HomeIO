Topic 6 Murphy, PML — §6.2.6 KL divergence

Same two distributions, two different answers

KL divergence isn't symmetric. When you squeeze a complicated distribution p into a simple family q, the direction you minimize decides what q looks like. Fit a single Gaussian to two bumps: one direction gives you a wide blur over both, the other gives you a tight fit to just one.

6.2.6.1

Whose weight is on the log ratio?

Both directions integrate the same log ratio. The only difference is which distribution weights it, and so where a mismatch gets punished.

Forward · inclusive · mean-seeking
KL(p‖q) = ∫ p log (p ⁄ q)

Weighted by p. Anywhere p has mass but q ≈ 0, log(p/q) blows up. So q is zero-avoiding: it must cover every mode, even if that means putting mass in the gaps. This is what maximum likelihood minimizes.

Reverse · exclusive · mode-seeking
KL(q‖p) = ∫ q log (q ⁄ p)

Weighted by q. Anywhere q has mass but p ≈ 0, log(q/p) blows up. So q is zero-forcing: it must stay out of p's empty regions, even if that means dropping whole modes. This is what variational inference minimizes.
