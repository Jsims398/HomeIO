Classify by multiplying simple clues

Is this email spam? Naive Bayes answers by asking two things: how common is spam in general, and how well does "spam" explain each word I see? One bold shortcut turns the whole thing into counting.

9.3

Naive Bayes in three steps

Step 1 · Flip the question
p(spam | email) ∝ p(spam) × p(email | spam)

"Is this spam?" is hard to answer directly. Bayes' rule flips it: if it were spam, how likely is an email like this? Do the same for normal mail, then compare the two scores.

Step 2 · The "naive" shortcut
p(email | spam) ≈ p(w1 | spam) × p(w2 | spam) × …

Pretend each word shows up on its own once you know the label. Not really true ("free" and "money" travel together), but with 20 words it shrinks 1,048,576 word combos down to 20 numbers per label.

Step 3 · Fitting = counting
θ = (# spam emails with "free") ⁄ (# spam emails)

The best-fit value (the MLE, §9.3.2) is just a fraction from the training data. No calculus, no iterations. Add 1 to every count (smoothing, §9.3.3) so no word ever gets probability exactly 0.

Why it matters: it trains in one pass, works with tiny datasets and thousands of features, and is still a go-to first model for text: spam filters, sentiment, topic tagging.

9.3.4 · Hidden connection to logistic regression
log-odds = bias + push1 + push2 + …

Taking logs turns the products into a sum of one weight per word. That is exactly the shape of logistic regression, so Naive Bayes draws a straight-line boundary too. The only difference is how it gets the weights: by counting, instead of by searching for the best ones.

9.4

Two ways to build a classifier

Generative · e.g. Naive Bayes
Learn p(x | y) and p(y), then flip with Bayes

Learn what each class looks like: a recipe that could write fake spam and fake normal mail. To classify, see which recipe better explains the email.

Discriminative · e.g. logistic regression
Learn p(y | x) directly

Skip describing the emails. Only learn the dividing line between the classes. Like telling cats from dogs by "pointy ears?" without ever learning to draw either.
